00 / Statement Artist Statement

A collection of five geometric narratives — eyes that wander, stories that see.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of geometric abstraction and illustration. My work begins with a simple observation: the wandering eye — always moving, always framing — is the origin of every image. To see is to compose. To compose is to tell a story.

Wandering Eyes is a collection of five works, each drawn with a small vocabulary of geometric primitives — circles, rectangles, triangles, quads, arcs, and lines. Some works read as illustrations (a crow beside a human, a column of vitamins, a dance studio); others as interior landscapes (a figure under a black sky, an eye that sees the world).

Each work carries a written story — a short prose piece that accompanies the composition. The stories range from whimsical ("if only walls could provide vitamins") to melancholic ("once, in a world cloaked beneath a perpetual black sky"). Together they form a set of small fables told in geometry and code.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

01 / Primitives

Geometric Vocabulary

Every work in this collection is drawn from a small set of geometric primitives: circles, rectangles, triangles, quads, arcs, and lines. There is no raster image, no imported texture, no photograph. Every shape is placed by code, so the work is legible at any scale — from a print to a wall.

02 / Story

Each Work Has One

Every work carries a written story — a short prose piece that accompanies the composition. The stories range from whimsical (a wall column that delivers vitamins) to melancholic (a solitary figure under a perpetual black sky). The collection is not just a set of images; it is a set of fables told in geometry.

03 / The Eye

Wandering Gaze

The collection is held together by the eye — the sense of sight, the act of looking, the wandering gaze. Some works literally contain eyes; others are about the act of seeing. The title comes from the sense that these are not drawings of objects, but of what the eye notices as it moves across a scene.
